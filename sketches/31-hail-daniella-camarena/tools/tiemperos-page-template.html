A living practice

Tiemperos (also graniceros or aureros) are ritual specialists in the villages around the volcanoes. They are chosen by lightning or by dreams. They climb to caves and crosses to feed Don Goyo (Popocatépetl) and Rosita (Iztaccíhuatl), pray in Nahuatl and ask for rain. Anthropologist Julio Glockner has documented these ceremonies since 1989.

What the weather does

The volcanoes do gather clouds. In the rainy season, moist air is pushed up their slopes, cools, and builds afternoon storms. The ritual calendar lines up with that season: crosses go up on 3 May as the rains begin, and thanksgiving comes in November when they end.

Next to the hail cannon

Both try to manage the same storms. The cannon tried to push clouds away from the cars. The tiemperos invite the clouds in, and say they turn only the hail aside, toward the ravines. Neither has been shown to change a cloud. One is a machine; the other is a relationship with the mountain.
