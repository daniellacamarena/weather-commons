The hail cannon at VW Puebla, 2018

San Lorenzo Almecatla, Cuautlancingo, Puebla · black-and-white line drawing · vertical scale in km above the ground, cannon and people enlarged

Which cloud was fired at

The target was the cumulonimbus, the only cloud that makes hail. In automatic mode a radar fired the cannon whenever a convective cloud started to build, so in practice it fired at growing cumulus congestus too. Press reports say "every time a cloud formed".

How many times

No firing log has been published. What is on record: 3 devices, one blast about every 7 seconds (514 per hour per cannon), automatic mode from May to 20 August 2018. The state permit allowed 2 hours a year, about 3,100 blasts. There were 66 days with rain in that period. If the cannons fired about 1 hour per storm, the total would be roughly 100,000 blasts. That figure is an estimate.

Why it could not reach the cloud

The blast is very loud at the mouth but spreads out and weakens to ordinary sound within tens of metres. Hail grows 3.5 to 6 km up, inside an updraft of 10 to 25 m/s. Studies have found no measurable effect on hail or rain.

Sources: Expansión, 15 and 20 Aug 2018 (3 devices, acetylene, every 7 s, 500 m claimed radius, 2 h/yr state permit, operating since May); Xataka México (≈120 dB); rainy days and July rainfall from ERA5 and CHIRPS at the plant (see the companion map). Altitudes and temperature levels are typical rainy-season values for Puebla, not measurements from a specific storm.
